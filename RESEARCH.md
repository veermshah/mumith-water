# Mumith Water — Research Notes

Mumith Water is a pre-launch idea. It has **no suppliers, partners, co-packers, sponsors, customers, or certifications**. Everything below is background research used to write the landing page. Companies named here are documented only as examples of what exists in the market — none of them has any relationship with Mumith Water.

Research compiled October 2026. Sources were checked at that time; re-verify before relying on any figure.

## 2. Materials we're exploring

No supplier or partnership exists for any of these. Status reflects what each company publicly says as of research date.

### 2a. PHA (polyhydroxyalkanoates)

- **What it is made from:** Polyesters produced by microbial fermentation of sugars/plant oils. Danimer Scientific makes PHA under the Nodax® name via a patented fermentation process.
- **Company status:**
  - **Danimer Scientific** filed Chapter 11 on 18 March 2025 (SEC 8-K: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1779020/000095017025041367/dnmr-20250318.htm). **Teknor Apex** acquired Danimer on 10 June 2025 (bankruptcy court winning bid: $19M); Danimer continues to operate as a separate entity under its own name (https://www.teknorapex.com/en-us/news/teknor-apex-acquires-danimer-scientific-to-further-diversify-sustainable-materials-portfolio).
  - **Cove** (Los Angeles) launched a PHA water bottle in 2022 but has since moved to **PB1**, a molded-fiber bottle with a thin bio-based barrier designed to be recycled with paper (https://cove.co/, https://cove.co/faq; Inc., Dec 2025: https://www.inc.com/sara-kiley-watson/this-startups-new-product-is-targeting-plastic-pollution-with-a-novel-kind-of-recyclable-packaging/91277036). Inc. reports the original PHA bottle was expensive and relied on inconsistent composting infrastructure. Cove therefore is **no longer a PHA bottle lead**.
- **How/where it breaks down:** Danimer states its PHA resin is certified by TÜV (formerly Vinçotte) as biodegradable in soil, compost, freshwater and marine environments, and that Nodax will also degrade in industrial and home compost (https://danimerscientific.com/pha-certifications/, https://danimerscientific.com/about-us/faqs/).
- **How long:** A meta-study of marine studies estimated a PHA **bottle has a mean lifetime of 1.5–3.5 years in the marine environment**; thin films 0.1–0.2 years (Dilkes-Hoffman et al., Marine Pollution Bulletin 142:15–24, 2019 — https://doi.org/10.1016/j.marpolbul.2019.03.020).
- **Cost/scale:** A 2022 review reported an average PHA price of about **US$8.0/kg** (2018 market study; $7.6/kg for packaging) vs about US$1.25–2.53/kg for polypropylene/polyethylene (RSC Advances, 2022 — https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlepdf/2022/ra/d1ra08796g). Danimer's bankruptcy illustrates commercial-scale risk.
- **Certifications (resin level, per manufacturer):** TÜV AUSTRIA "OK" biodegradable schemes (soil, water, marine) and compost; FDA food-contact approval. Certification applies to the certified resin/product, not to any bottle Mumith would make. TÜV's OK biodegradable MARINE scheme explicitly says it does not promote discarding items in the sea (https://be.tuvaustria.com/wp-content/uploads/sites/73/2024/03/CS-OK12-EN_biodegradable_MARINE.pdf).
- **Limitations:** Cost; supply concentration; a finished bottle (cap, label, ink, barrier) would need its own testing and certification; "marine biodegradable" still means years, not weeks.

**Chosen as the visual reference for the 3D bottle** (matte, warm, natural-toned) because it is the only material in this list whose manufacturer claims biodegradation across soil, freshwater and marine settings as well as compost.

### 2b. PLA (polylactic acid) — NatureWorks Ingeo™

- **Made from:** Lactic acid fermented from plant sugars; NatureWorks' new Thailand plant uses locally sourced sugarcane (https://natureworksllc.com/about/news/press-releases/2026-04-29-natureworks-announces-grand-opening-thailand).
- **Status:** Operating. Opened a second, fully integrated Ingeo plant in Nakhon Sawan, Thailand on 29 April 2026 with ~75,000 t/yr capacity (same press release).
- **How/where it breaks down:** Industrial composting conditions (sustained ~58 °C). ASTM D6400 requires ≥90% disintegration in 12 weeks and ≥90% biodegradation in 180 days at those conditions. PLA degrades very slowly in soil, seawater and landfill (Infinita Lab technical summary — https://infinitalab.com/blog/pla-biodegradability-compostability-testing/; Scientific Reports 2025 disintegration study — https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-91647-z).
- **Limitations:** Low heat resistance — heat deflection temperature ~55–65 °C (International Journal of Biological Macromolecules review — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0141813022015239); can contaminate PET recycling; lower barrier/inertness than PET (Fraunhofer IVV — https://www.ivv.fraunhofer.de/content/dam/ivv/en/documents/Forschungsfelder/Produktsicherheit-und-analytik/Sorption_and_migration_behavior_of_polylactic_acid.pdf).
- **Certifications:** Product-specific; would need verifying for a specific grade/bottle. Not claimed on site.

### 2c. Seaweed-based packaging — Notpla (Ooho)

- **Made from:** Brown seaweed and plant extracts (Notpla — https://www.notpla.com/ooho; Circle Economy Knowledge Hub — https://knowledge-hub.circle-economy.com/article/23209).
- **Status:** Operating; Ooho used at events, e.g., replacing cups at the 2019 London Marathon (https://www.notpla.com/magazine-posts/the-story-of-ooho-how-an-edible-bubble).
- **How/where it breaks down:** Notpla says its materials are home compostable, breaking down in about **4–6 weeks** (https://www.notpla.com/magazine-posts/home-compostable-vs-industrial-compostable-packaging-whats-the-difference/). Ooho is edible.
- **Format:** Ooho holds roughly **20–100 mL** per capsule (MIT Solve — https://solve.mit.edu/solutions/15678) — a pod, not a bottle.
- **Limitations:** Short shelf life; Oohos "have to be produced in close vicinity of where they are used" (Circle Economy). Not a resealable bottle.
- **Certifications:** Notpla states its coated packaging (not Ooho specifically) is BPI-certified industrial compostable for certain SKUs and tested to EN 13432, ASTM D6400 and ISO 20200 (https://envirowise.eco/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Notpla_QA.pdf). We do not claim any certification for Ooho.

### 2d. Paper-based bottles — Paboco

- **Made from:** Primary FSC®-certified wood fibers with an integrated barrier and paper-threaded neck (https://www.paboco.com/).
- **Status:** Operating; majority-owned by ALPLA (Packaging Insights, Apr 2024). Began full-scale production in 2024 (Packaging Europe — https://packagingeurope.com/news/paboco-commences-full-scale-production-for-fibre-based-bottles/11005.article); launched a paper bottle + fiber cap with Blue Ocean Closures in Oct 2024.
- **How it is disposed:** Paboco describes it as **recyclable as paper packaging** — not as compostable or biodegradable.
- **Limitations:** Needs a barrier layer; earlier trials (Carlsberg) used a PEF bio-based coating (packaging journal — https://packaging-journal.de/en/paboco-paper-bottle-consumer-test/).

### 2e. Plant-based cartons — Tetra Pak, Boxed Water

- **Made from:** Paperboard plus thin polymer layers (some plant-based, e.g., sugarcane polyethylene) and, for aseptic cartons, aluminum (https://www.tetrapak.com/en-us/solutions/packaging/packaging-material/renewable-materials).
- **Disposal:** Recyclable where carton recycling exists — **not** home or industrially compostable. Boxed Water says its cartons can be recycled via curbside, drop-off or mail-in programs (https://boxedwater.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/26514373990803).
- **Claims caution:** In 2025 BBB National Programs' NAD recommended Boxed Water clarify or discontinue some "renewable"/"plant-based" claims built on mass-balance accounting (Food Dive — https://www.fooddive.com/news/boxed-water-is-better-marketing-sustainability/751069/). A useful precedent for careful wording.

### Infrastructure reality check

- BioCycle's 2023 survey found **14.9 million US households** have municipally supported food-waste collection access (curbside or drop-off) (https://www.biocycle.net/residential-food-waste-collection-access-in-u-s/). Composting policy is set at state and local level (EPA — https://www.epa.gov/sustainable-management-food/composting).

## 3. Water sourcing (Texas)

### Regulation
- **FDA** regulates bottled water as a food (not EPA). Standards of identity, standards of quality and bottled-water CGMPs apply (FDA — https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/bottled-water-everywhere-keeping-it-safe; CDC — https://cdc.gov/drinking-water/about/drinking-water-standards-and-regulations-an-overview.html).
- **21 CFR 165.110** defines bottled water types (spring, purified, mineral, etc.) and quality standards (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-165/subpart-B/section-165.110).
- **21 CFR Part 129** sets CGMP for processing and bottling; product water must come from an approved source (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-129).
- FDA's lead limit for bottled water is **5 ppb** (vs. EPA's 15 ppb action level for tap water) (FDA page above).
- **Texas DSHS:** firms that process and bottle water in sealed containers must hold a **Food Manufacturer license**; governing rules include Texas Health & Safety Code Ch. 431, **25 TAC §229.81–91** and 21 CFR 129 (https://www.dshs.texas.gov/food-manufacturers-wholesalers-warehouses/bottled-vended-water-food-manufacturers-wholesalers-warehouses). 25 TAC §229.81 adopts 21 CFR 165.110 by reference (https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/texas/25-Tex-Admin-Code-SS-229-81).
- **License fees** are tiered by gross annual sales, from **$100 (two-year license, <$10k sales)** up to $1,680 (≥$10M) for food manufacturers/private labelers (DSHS FY25 Fee Resource Manual — https://www.dshs.texas.gov/sites/default/files/legislative/2025-Reports/fy25-fee-resource-manual-final.pdf; 25 TAC §229.182).

### Spring vs. purified
- **Spring water:** from an underground formation that flows naturally to the surface; must be collected at the spring or via a borehole tapping that formation, with the same composition as the natural flow (FDA).
- **Purified water:** produced by distillation, deionization, reverse osmosis or similar, meeting the USP "purified water" definition (21 CFR 165.110). Source can be a municipal supply (FDA; California CDPH FAQ notes TDS < 10 ppm — https://www.cdph.ca.gov/Programs/CEH/DFDCS/Pages/FDBPrograms/FoodSafetyProgram/WaterFAQs.aspx).
- **Cost:** We did not find a credible public source quantifying the price difference. See Open questions.

### Texas co-packers / private-label bottlers (verified to exist; no contact made)
- **Eventide Water** — operates multiple production lines in **Mineral Wells, TX**; private-label co-packing since 2006; offers municipal-source, spring, carbonated and bulk water; lines up to 400 bottles/min (http://www.eventidewater.com/about-us/, http://www.eventidewater.com/capabilities/).
- **Aqua Bottling** — says it operates a Texas factory handling label production, filling and shipping for custom-labeled water; purified via filtration and reverse osmosis (https://aquabottling.com/aqua-bottling-your-source-for-high-quality-bottled-water-solutions/).
- Neither has been contacted. Neither has confirmed it can fill non-PET (PHA, fiber) bottles — this is the key unknown.

### Approach summarized on site
Start with a licensed Texas co-packer rather than building a plant; likely begin with purified water (approved municipal source + RO) because spring water depends on access to a qualifying spring; confirm a co-packer can run a non-PET bottle on its line; Mumith itself would need a DSHS license if it acts as a manufacturer/private labeler.

## 4. Claim wording (FTC Green Guides, 16 CFR Part 260)

- **§260.7 Compostable:** need competent and reliable evidence that all materials become usable compost in a timely manner; qualify claims if the item can't be home-composted or if facilities aren't available to a substantial majority of consumers (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-260/section-260.7).
- **§260.8 Degradable/biodegradable:** unqualified claims require the entire item to decompose within a reasonably short time after customary disposal; it is deceptive for solid-waste items that won't decompose within **one year**, and landfills/incinerators/recycling don't provide those conditions (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-260/section-260.8).
- **§260.4** — avoid general claims like "eco-friendly."

**Site rules adopted:** say "designed to break down," name the environment and timeframe with a source, say "exploring" rather than "using," never show a certification logo, never say "100% biodegradable" or "eco-friendly."
